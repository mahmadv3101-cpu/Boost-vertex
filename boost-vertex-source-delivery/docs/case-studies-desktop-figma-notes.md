# Case Studies Listing — Desktop Figma Notes

## Full-frame overview

The supplied desktop frame is a dark, 1280px-wide Boost Vertex listing page with a compact shared navigation, a lime-curve hero, dense results-card grid, a high-contrast lime summary band, industry-expertise cards, a centered editorial quote area, a final conversion block, and a compact multi-column footer. The mobile design is visible beside it only as a secondary reference and is out of scope for this desktop pass.

## Confirmed visual direction

The desktop route continues the existing obsidian and electric-lime Boost Vertex system. Its dominant page-specific motif is a broad muted-lime organic arc behind the centered hero title, followed by data-forward modular cards rather than image-led case studies. The primary display copy reads **“Proven Performance. Quantifiable Growth.”**

Further notes will record only text and details verified from closer Figma inspection; unverified testimonial copy, figures, or client claims will not be invented.

## Hero calibration correction

At readable scale, the desktop hero curve is a single broad muted-lime ellipse that enters flush from the upper-left beneath the header, then descends diagonally to leave a large open obsidian field around the lower-right copy and controls. It is not a full rounded blob or a border outline. The display heading uses Chivo with less compressed character spacing than the initial implementation. The white first line remains strong and upright; `Quantifiable` and `Growth.` are solid lime, in the intended italic display treatment, with tight but readable line separation.

## Fast Track and curve correction

The Figma desktop hero carries the Fast Track control inside its right edge, not only in the Home hero. It consists of a small lime `Fast Track` label, then a narrow dark rounded vertical tray with four separated line-icon actions. The curve occupies the upper-left hero plane as a shallow broad olive ellipse: its upper field reaches much farther across the hero than the prior implementation, while its lower curve exits the left side substantially higher. The curve must therefore be widened and lifted rather than simply moved downward.
